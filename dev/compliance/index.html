    Compliance¶

    Last reviewed: 22 October 2026. This is an engineering map of the US and EU rules that touch Mohar, written so the design meets them from the start. It isn't legal advice. Anyone deploying Mohar in a regulated setting should have counsel confirm how these rules apply to them.

    Two different questions run through this page:

    • Rules on the project. What applies to Mohar itself, as open-source software published by an individual with no revenue.
    • Rules on the people who use it. What Mohar's users have to prove, and how Mohar's receipts help them prove it.

    Summary¶

    Rule Applies to the project now? How Mohar helps users
    EU AI Act No. Mohar records and gates other systems' actions; it isn't itself a high-risk AI system. Automatic, lifetime event logs (Article 12) and human oversight (Article 14) for the agents it governs.
    GDPR (EU) and CCPA (California) Only for the project's own website, which collects no personal data. Pseudonymous receipts, no raw arguments, erasure by key destruction, configurable retention.
    EU Cyber Resilience Act Not as a manufacturer while it's non-commercial open source. Would apply if a company sells it. A documented vulnerability process and SBOMs from day one, so the move to manufacturer duties is small.
    NIS2 and DORA (EU) No. Tamper-evident records of who changed what in critical systems.
    US export controls (EAR) Checked: publicly available source using standard cryptography is not subject to the EAR. Not applicable.
    NIST AI RMF, NIST SP 800-53 (US) Voluntary frameworks. Evidence for audit and accountability controls.
    SOC 2, ISO/IEC 27001, ISO/IEC 42001 Certifications for organisations, not code. Evidence for logging, change management and AI governance controls.
    HIPAA (US) No. Audit controls for systems that touch health data.

    EU AI Act¶

    The Digital Omnibus on AI, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force since 27 July 2026, moved the high-risk obligations: stand-alone high-risk systems listed in Annex III now apply from 2 December 2027, and AI that is a safety component of regulated products (Annex I) from 2 August 2028.

    What Mohar's receipts are built to support:

    • Article 12, record-keeping. High-risk systems must "technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system", with logs that help identify risk, support post-market monitoring and monitor operation. Every Mohar decision is logged automatically, chained, and signed, so a deployer can show the log is complete and unaltered.
    • Article 14, human oversight. People must be able to understand, override and stop the system. Escalation sends out-of-bounds actions to a named person, the approval is bound to the exact action, and the person's identity is in the receipt.

    Mohar doesn't make an agent compliant on its own. It gives the deployer the records and controls the Act asks for.

    GDPR and US state privacy laws¶

    An append-only log and a right to erasure (GDPR Article 17) pull in opposite directions. Mohar resolves this in the design rather than later:

    1. No personal data in the chained fields. Receipts identify people and agents by pseudonymous identifiers (human:7f3a...), never by name or email. The mapping from identifier to person lives in a separate, mutable directory file.
    2. Arguments are hashed, never stored. args_sha256 proves which action was approved without keeping its contents.
    3. Erasure by destroying the mapping. Deleting a person's directory entry, and any per-person key used to encrypt optional notes, leaves the chain verifiable but no longer linked to that person. This approach (often called crypto-shredding) is the common answer for immutable logs; whether it fully satisfies Article 17 hasn't been settled by regulators, so deployers should confirm with their DPO.
    4. Retention is a setting. Old segments can be archived or dropped after a signed checkpoint, so the remaining chain still verifies from that checkpoint.
    5. Local by default. The log lives on the deployer's machine. Nothing is sent to the project.

    The project website sets no cookies, runs no analytics and collects nothing.

    EU Cyber Resilience Act¶

    Manufacturers must report actively exploited vulnerabilities from 11 September 2026, and the full product requirements apply from 11 December 2027. Free open-source software that isn't placed on the market in a commercial activity falls outside manufacturer obligations. An organisation that sells or monetises Mohar later would become a manufacturer for that product.

    Already in place, so that move is small:

    • a security policy with private vulnerability reporting (SECURITY.md);
    • automated secret scanning, CodeQL and dependency updates;
    • planned for the first release: a software bill of materials and signed build provenance for every artefact.

    US export controls¶

    Under 15 CFR 742.15(b), publicly available encryption source code is not subject to the EAR. A notification to BIS and the NSA is required only for code that performs non-standard cryptography. Mohar uses only standard, published algorithms (Ed25519, SHA-256), so no notification applies. Any change that adds a non-standard cryptographic algorithm must revisit this.

    How the project itself is built¶

    The development practices auditors look for under ISO/IEC 27001 A.8.25 to A.8.29 (secure development) and SOC 2 CC8:

    • every change starts from an issue and lands through a reviewed pull request; main is protected with 14 required checks, linear history and no force pushes;
    • static analysis (CodeQL, clippy with warnings denied), secret scanning (gitleaks), dependency advisories and licences (cargo-deny), workflow security (zizmor), OpenSSF Scorecard;
    • tests on three operating systems, an 85% coverage floor, mutation checks on security rules;
    • signed-off commits (DCO), release tags limited to admins, attested release artefacts;
    • private vulnerability reporting and fix-before-disclosure (SECURITY.md).

    Open questions for counsel¶

    • Does crypto-shredding of the identity mapping satisfy GDPR Article 17 for a given deployment?
    • When a company is formed and offers a paid tier, which CRA conformity route applies?
    • For AI Act Annex III deployments, which minimum log retention applies to the deployer, and does it conflict with a shorter privacy retention setting?
